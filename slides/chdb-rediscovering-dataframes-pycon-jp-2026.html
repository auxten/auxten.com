Hiroshima okonomiyaki is layered, never mixed — and so is this. Your pandas calls are the sauce on top; underneath, each call is recorded into a lazy chain, compiled into ONE SQL plan, and executed by the columnar engine only when you actually look at the result — len, print, iteration. That's why chained pandas ops stop costing a copy each: the layers cook as one. (Architecture diagram with the full detail: auxten.com slides repo.)

Ranged reads: you pay for the columns you touch — not the file.
Why "reading from S3 takes longer than the query" stops being true: the engine reads the Parquet footer first — schema plus statistics — then fetches only the column chunks the query touches, via HTTP Range requests. len() on 2.77M remote rows in 3.3 s without downloading. And streaming execution never materializes the full table: 344 MB peak versus pandas' 975 MB on the same file. Fair caveat for the notes: on very high-latency links, many small range requests can lose to one big download.
